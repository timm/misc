# Nomination Statement for TCSE Distinguished Education Award

## Q1. Direct Educational Contributions

For more than a decade, in the United States and Sweden, Gregory has made sustained contributions to software engineering education through pioneering curriculum design (Criterion 2.2), internationally adopted teaching materials (Criterion 2.3, see Q2), and leadership in the education community (Criterion 2.4). He has taught software engineering since 2012 — at the University of Minnesota, the University of South Carolina (2015–2019), and since 2019 at Chalmers University of Technology and the University of Gothenburg — reaching thousands of students across nine courses.

*Pioneering curriculum design (Criterion 2.2).* As Program Manager for two software engineering M.Sc. programs at Chalmers and the University of Gothenburg since 2023, he leads curricula serving hundreds of students each year. This year he designed two master's programs launching in 2027: a modernized Software Engineering program and "Software and AI Engineering", the first program in Sweden dedicated to AI Engineering (SE4AI). It offers a model for integrating AI engineering into software engineering curricula.

*Bridging research and practice.* He introduced courses that place students directly in research and industry: the Research Project in Software Engineering, research seminars in AI and Software Engineering, and the Industrial Practice Project, in which master's students earn credit for internships. Building that course required extensive industrial outreach, and it has increased the number of students completing research-based theses with industrial partners.

*Leadership (Criterion 2.4).* Since 2025, he has co-chaired the International Workshop on evaLuation and assEssment in softwARe eNgineers Education and tRaining (LEARNER). He has also been an invited panelist at the ICSE 2020 Student Mentoring Workshop and the ICST 2023 Doctoral Symposium.

*Recognition.* 2026 Chalmers Pedagogical Prize (co-recipient) [1]; 2018 University of South Carolina Graduate Teaching Award.

## Q2. Educational Materials and Resources

*Openly disseminated, internationally adopted materials (Criterion 2.3).* Gregory publicly releases complete materials for every course he teaches. His teaching website [2] hosts materials for 22 course offerings across nine courses from 2015 to 2026, including Software Quality and Testing (seven offerings, 2020–2026), Software Testing and Quality Assurance, Software Engineering (graduate and undergraduate), Software Architecture, Software Engineering Principles for Complex Systems, Team Programming, and Mobile and Web Development.

Each course site combines a syllabus with explicit learning objectives, lecture slides and notes, hands-on exercises, team-project assignments, tool tutorials, and example source code. Because the materials are modular and freely available, an instructor can adopt a single lecture, an assignment, or an entire course. His software testing course, for example, covers quality assurance, test design at unit and system level, test automation, and formal verification, and is updated each year to reflect advances in the field such as AI-assisted testing.

His software testing materials in particular have been adopted or adapted by educators worldwide, including in South Korea (KAIST), the United States (University of Minnesota), Australia (University of New South Wales), Sweden (Lund University, Mälardalen University, Blekinge Institute of Technology), and Luxembourg (University of Luxembourg), among others. This reach across four continents shows that his syllabi, assignments, and team projects serve as models well beyond his own institutions.

## Q3. Mentorship

Gregory has served as main or co-supervisor of Ph.D. students in Sweden and the United States: three have graduated, one left after earning a Licentiate degree (a Swedish intermediate degree, roughly half of a Ph.D.), and three are in progress. He deliberately involves his Ph.D. students as co-supervisors of Bachelor's and Master's theses and as co-teachers, so that they develop their own pedagogical skills — training the next generation of software engineering educators. His graduated students have gone on to positions in academia and industry (Google).

He has supervised 30 Bachelor's and Master's research theses and examined more than 50 others. He has published 20 articles with Bachelor's or Master's students as co-authors (e.g., [3–5]), developing their skills as junior researchers. These students have gone on to Ph.D. studies and to careers at companies including Amazon Web Services, Zenseact/Volvo Cars, and Volvo Group. Beyond his own students, he has served as an external examiner or committee member for ten Ph.D. candidates at universities in seven countries, including TU Delft, the University of Passau, the University of Sheffield, and Monash University.

Beyond formal supervision, he has individually mentored many of the students in his courses and programs as they prepared for — or developed — their careers, offering personal guidance as they overcame technical and personal challenges.
