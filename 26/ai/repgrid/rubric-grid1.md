# Repertory Grid 1 — Rubric (provost-derived constructs)

Elements (16): NCSU CSC; 12 BOG-approved peers (Georgia Tech, Michigan State, Purdue, Rutgers–New Brunswick, Texas A&M, Arizona, UC Davis, Georgia, Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Maryland, Minnesota, Virginia Tech) CS/computing departments; Duke CS (local rival, Tim's request); IDEAL (Pfaendtner's implied ideal department, scored from interview text).

Scale: 1 = left (ideal) pole, 5 = right (contrast) pole. Distance-from-ideal = Σ|score − ideal score| per element.

Scoring evidence ladder (strongest first): (1) program/catalog pages, (2) course-catalog descriptions, (3) public syllabi, (4) dept news / industry-partnership pages. Every cell = score + one-line evidence + URL. No public signal → score 3 + flag "no signal", never silent guess.

## Constructs

### C1. AI dosed early and often ... AI quarantined in late electives
- 1 = required AI/ML touchpoint in year 1 + reinforced in ≥2 later required courses
- 3 = AI required once in core; elsewhere optional
- 5 = AI reachable only via senior/grad electives
- Source: Pfaendtner, RHRQ Summer 2026, p.9–10: "where does the dose of AI come early on and then where do we keep redosing it to reinforce it"; "comprehensive freshman experience".

### C2. Fundamentals-first ... tool-chasing
- 1 = AI courses require/teach theory, algorithms, critical evaluation before tool use
- 3 = mixed; some fundamentals prerequisites
- 5 = curriculum leads with vendor tools/prompting, little required theory
- Source: Pfaendtner p.10: "you can't replace the time and space a college student has to learn fundamentals and critical thinking"; "engineering-forward, computer-science-forward way, so the fundamentals are still there".

### C3. Parsimony: small/cheap/open models in teaching ... frontier-model & GPU dependence
- 1 = courses built on small/local/open models, cost-aware compute; students run models themselves
- 3 = mix of API calls and local models
- 5 = teaching depends on paid frontier APIs / large cluster access
- Sources: Pfaendtner p.17: "parsimony — having small language models that do bespoke tasks with way less power usage. That's going to be transformational... the way we educate"; Menzies, "Sophisticated AI: Simple Ain't Stupid" deck; EZR paper (arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03640).

### C4. Doing: projects + industry embedded ... talking: lecture-only
- 1 = required project courses with external/industry stakeholders; co-ops; capstones with real clients
- 3 = capstone exists, internal projects only
- 5 = lecture/exam dominant, no required project work
- Source: Pfaendtner, article title "Stop talking, start doing"; p.15: "We need to stop saying 'You have to do AI' and dig into 'What are you doing?'"; p.12 industry partnership two-way street.

### C5. Open source & reproducibility culture ... closed/proprietary
- 1 = open-source tools required in courses; public course repos; reproducibility taught
- 3 = open tools used, nothing published
- 5 = proprietary stack (MATLAB-style licensing), closed materials
- Source: Pfaendtner p.13–14: "open source means we publish our work first"; "we also have to have open source software, open source research in AI"; p.15 MATLAB→Python story.

### C6. Faculty AI-upskilling structures ... none visible
- 1 = named program (fellowships/sabbaticals/training) for faculty AI upskilling
- 3 = ad-hoc workshops only
- 5 = no public signal of faculty AI development
- Source: Pfaendtner p.11: "faculty fellowship program so people can step away on their sabbatical with the explicit purpose of upskilling in AI".

### C7. Serves whole college/university ... siloed to own majors
- 1 = CS-run AI service courses/minor open to all majors, large non-major enrollment
- 3 = minor exists with restrictive prerequisites
- 5 = AI courses restricted to CS majors
- Sources: Pfaendtner p.9: 1,850 freshmen — "How are we teaching them to think about AI?"; lead.pdf notes: applied AI intro sequence CSC 202/301 "general to eng".

### C8. Ships AI credentials ... stalled/none
- 1 = BS-in-AI (or equivalent) + minor + MS all live and enrolling
- 3 = one credential live, others in pipeline
- 5 = no AI-named credential
- Source: Pfaendtner p.9: "being agile is essential"; lead.pdf notes: NCSU BS-in-AI "on hold".

### C9. AI rigor: testing/eval/security of AI systems taught ... ML content only
- 1 = required coursework on testing, evaluation, security, reliability of AI systems
- 3 = topics appear inside electives
- 5 = only model-building content, no systems-rigor coursework
- Sources: lead.pdf notes (Will: "pressure test systems, security... teach fundamentals of security"; Dominic: "AI will turn SE into actual engineers"); Menzies "Solutions" slide: "More testing, of everything, always"; Pfaendtner via Griffin exchange p.10 (debugging/security fundamentals affirmed "100%").

### C10 (†). AI ownership settled ... turf-locked
- 1 = one unit clearly owns AI credentials; campus accepts it (credentials live, no competing claims)
- 3 = multiple units run parallel AI credentials, mild overlap/contest
- 5 = credential proposals blocked, stalled, or forcibly reconfigured by inter-unit contest
- Source: elicited from T. Menzies, triad 5 ({Maryland, Minnesota} vs NCSU, 2026-08-04); scored from dossier facts (which unit runs each credential; NCSU BS-in-AI "on hold"/joint-ECE-ISE evidence, lead.pdf notes).

### C11 (†). Institutional AI bet made ... no visible push
- 1 = named university-level AI investment with money attached (institute, hiring wave, flagship facility) — e.g., Purdue Computes/IPAI, GT AI Makerspace, UGA 50–70-faculty hiring initiative
- 3 = committee/initiative level, modest funding
- 5 = no visible institutional AI investment
- Source: elicited from T. Menzies, triad 6 ({Georgia Tech, Illinois} vs Rutgers); scored from dossier institutional-partnership evidence.

Note: † constructs imported from triadic elicitation (grid 2); remaining four elicited constructs retired — brand duplicates public rankings, land-grant constant across public peers, others carried no new variance.
