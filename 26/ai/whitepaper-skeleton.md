# The Future of AI at NC State CSC
## Stop Talking, Start Doing — a one-page agenda

*(Skeleton v0.1 — each section: purpose, key content, source ammunition. Target: one page + one exhibit appendix.)*

---

### 1. Thesis (2 sentences)

> NC State has a world-class computer science department embedded in a world-class college of engineering — the Provost calls that combination "rare." CSC proposes to convert that rarity into leadership: teach every NC State engineer rigorous, affordable AI, starting this semester.

- Opens with the Provost's own words (RHRQ Summer 2026 interview) — frames everything after as *executing his vision*, not asking permission for ours.
- Note deliberate move: no claim to "own AI." Claim = teach everyone's AI rigor.

### 2. The moment (3 sentences)

Purpose: urgency without alarmism.

- Every peer on the BOG-approved list is moving: Purdue ships a BS in AI; Arizona ships BS + minor; Georgia Tech runs two AI minors and an NVIDIA-backed AI Makerspace; Duke puts required AI into its CS core Fall 2026; Maryland has BS/BA in AI arriving 2026–27. *(cite comparison grid, appendix)*
- NC State's counters are real but partial: AI concentration with year-1 CSC 201, 30-year corporate capstone — yet no AI minor, no standalone credential, campus ownership unsettled.
- The Provost's own diagnosis: universities are "built like tanks... yet at this moment, being agile is essential."

### 3. The problem industrial AI has — and why it is a CSC problem (4 bullets)

Purpose: differentiate. This is the section nobody else on campus can write.

- **Unsustainable costs** — the field must "mix and match expensive and cheap methods to survive." Provost agrees in print: parsimony and "small language models that do bespoke tasks with way less power usage... transformational... the way we educate."
- **Poor testing** — bugs surface months late; maintainability and security attack vectors unaddressed. Industry panel input (advisory board notes): "we cannot patch our way out of this."
- **Workforce deskilling** — where will senior engineers come from? Provost: fundamentals can't be replaced; the brain is "hardening."
- **Punchline**: these are computer-science problems — testing, evaluation, security, algorithmic economy. Departments that only *use* AI cannot fix them. The department that *studies* them must teach them.

### 4. What CSC delivers (the offer — 4 items, each one line + status)

Purpose: doing, not talking. Every item has a running artifact or near-approved status. No new committees required.

1. **CSC 202/301 applied-AI service sequence** for all engineering freshmen — the "dose early, redose often" architecture the Provost asked for. Status: 202 launching; 301 in design. *(resource note: service SCH → staff lines)*
2. **MS+AI track** — status: widespread approval; final push this semester.
3. **Rigor curriculum** — testing/eval/security-of-AI modules (Trustworthy AI for SE, DL Beyond Accuracy) promoted from electives toward required learning objectives (course-level edits; no degree paperwork).
4. **Parsimony lab practice** — students run small/open models on cheap hardware (EZR toolkit, active learning: labels ≈ √N); aligned with Red Hat's small-model direction and the Provost's stated excitement about it. Demos exist today (CS510 requirements-engineering projects).

### 5. What we ask (3 lines)

Purpose: cheap, specific, first-100-days-sized.

- CSC seats in the first cohort of the faculty AI-upskilling fellowships (program already budgeted).
- Provost's public endorsement of the 202/301 sequence as the college-wide freshman AI dose.
- Staff-line conversation indexed to service-course enrollment growth — nothing up front.

### 6. Close (2 sentences)

> The question on every campus is "who owns AI?" We think that is the wrong question. The right one — the Provost's own — is "What are you doing? How can we work together?" Here is what CSC is doing; here is how to multiply it.
